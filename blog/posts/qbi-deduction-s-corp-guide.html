S Corporation QBI Deduction: 2026 Limits and a Shareholder Worksheet

The qualified business income deduction is a shareholder-level deduction, not a 20% reduction in the S corporation’s tax bill or a guaranteed 20% tax saving. Identify qualified income, the return’s taxable-income limitation and any business-specific restrictions before assigning a deduction.

Section 199A continues after 2025

Current section 199A, as amended by the 2025 law, has no scheduled 2028 sunset. It generally provides a deduction based on qualified business income for eligible noncorporate taxpayers, subject to limits. A deduction amount is different from the tax saved; the tax effect depends on the rest of the return.

Start with qualified income, not cash distributions

The IRS QBI overview defines QBI using qualified income, gain, deductions and losses. A cash distribution is not the measure, and every Schedule K-1 item is not automatically QBI. Obtain the accompanying section 199A information and review relevant shareholder-level adjustments.

Regulation 1.199A-3 excludes reasonable compensation received from an S corporation from the owner’s QBI. The corporation’s properly allocable, otherwise deductible compensation expense reduces its business income. If the K-1 profit already reflects that expense, do not subtract the same salary again. Reconcile the starting figure before calculating 20%.

The IRS shareholder compensation guidance requires a facts-based wage review. A desire to increase QBI is not evidence that a lower salary is reasonable. The payroll comparison explains why employer and employee tax costs must also be considered.

Use the correct 2026 thresholds and phase-in endpoints

Revenue Procedure 2025-32, section 4.26, supplies these amounts for tax years beginning in 2026. Use taxable income before the QBI deduction, not business revenue, adjusted gross income or salary alone.

The phase-in interval is $150,000 for joint returns and $75,000 for other returns. These are not the older $100,000/$50,000 intervals. The table gives endpoints, not an automatic deduction for every taxpayer below them.

Wages, property and service-business rules can limit the amount

When fully applicable, section 199A’s wage/property limitation uses the greater of 50% of qualifying W-2 wages or 25% of those wages plus 2.5% of qualifying property UBIA. The business component is still limited by the QBI calculation. Employee wages and owner wages must satisfy the relevant definitions; a payroll total alone is not the final deduction.

Specified service trades or businesses face additional income-based restrictions. Identify the actual activity rather than assuming a new entity name changes its character. Separate entities, aggregation or extra payroll do not automatically create a benefit; additional wages also reduce business profit and cost cash.

The taxable-income cap is a separate calculation

The Form 8995 instructions explain the general overall limit: 20% × (taxable income before the QBI deduction − net capital gain including qualified dividends for this purpose). The linked instructions are the 2025 edition; use the current statute and the 2026 revenue procedure above for the 2026 rules and amounts.

Section 199A(i) adds a $400 minimum for an applicable taxpayer with at least $1,000 of aggregate QBI from active qualified businesses in 2026, with a material-participation condition. That is a separate rule, not a universal deduction for everyone with a K-1. The fictional deductions below exceed that amount, so this minimum does not change their arithmetic.

Original fictional shareholder worksheet: keep salary and the cap separate

Assume a single filer in a 2026 tax year receives $80,000 of W-2 salary and $120,000 of K-1 ordinary business profit from an S corporation. Assume the profit already reflects the corporation’s wage expense, and the entire $120,000 is supported QBI with no further adjustments or carryforwards. Salary is not QBI; subtracting it again would incorrectly turn $120,000 into $40,000.

For each alternative below, taxable income before the QBI deduction is a supplied whole-return input after the other return items and deductions. It is not calculated by adding salary and K-1 profit alone. Assume no REIT/PTP component, cooperative adjustment or other business, and no applicable wage/SSTB restriction because taxable income is below the single-filer threshold. All facts are invented; eligibility is assumed for arithmetic, not established.

In the second alternative, ($150,000 − $50,000) × 20% = $20,000, which is below the $24,000 QBI amount. That produces a $20,000 modeled deduction under the assumptions, not $20,000 of cash savings. In the third alternative, a blank capital-gain input is unknown, not zero, so the result is left unresolved.

Keep a return-level review record

Download a blank QBI review record (CSV). Preserve the tax year and filing status, K-1 statement, wage reconciliation, qualified adjustments, taxable income, capital-gain input and relevant restrictions before recording a conclusion.

The instructions also distinguish QBI loss carryforwards from losses suspended under other tax provisions. Keep their character and release year separate; a change in stock basis is not proof that every loss is immediately included in QBI. Continue with shareholder basis tracking and S-election costs and readiness for related questions.
