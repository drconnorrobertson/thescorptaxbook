S Corporation Stock and Debt Basis: Track Distributions and Loss Limits

An S corporation’s K-1 loss does not automatically become a current deduction on the shareholder’s return. Track stock basis and qualifying debt basis, then review the other loss restrictions. Basis is a tax measure; it is not the company’s bank balance or a substitute for the separate at-risk calculation.

Basis is the first loss limitation, not the last

Section 1366(d) limits the losses and deductions taken into account by reference to adjusted stock and qualifying debt basis. Amounts that fail this test generally carry forward under its rules. Passing it does not establish that the full amount offsets wages or other income now.

The IRS stock-and-debt basis guidance identifies four shareholder loss limitations, applied in order: basis, at-risk, passive activity and excess business loss. Keep the amount and character suspended by each provision separate. A basis carryforward and a passive-loss carryforward are different records.

Reconcile stock basis before considering the loss

Begin with the shareholder’s supported tax basis, not an assumed share of corporate assets. Section 1367 governs adjustments. The IRS guidance explains the general annual sequence: income increases, distributions, nondeductible noncapital items, then losses and deductions. Elections and special items can change a detailed calculation, so review the actual Form 7203 instructions.

Section 1368 separates distribution treatment from loss capacity. For a corporation without accumulated earnings and profits, a distribution generally uses stock basis, with the excess treated as gain from a sale or exchange. Debt basis does not make an excess stock distribution tax-free. Accumulated earnings and profits can change the distribution analysis.

Qualifying debt basis needs more than a guarantee

Regulation 1.1366-2 requires bona fide corporate indebtedness running directly to the shareholder. Merely guaranteeing the company’s bank loan does not create debt basis. Actual payments under a guarantee can change the analysis, so retain the payment trail and resulting legal rights rather than treating every guarantee as permanently irrelevant.

Keep debt principal and adjusted tax basis separate. Losses can reduce the latter without reducing the amount legally owed. Under section 1367, subsequent net increases can restore previously reduced debt basis before increasing stock basis. Timing, repayment and multiple-loan facts matter; do not simply add new income to every balance.

Original fictional example: distribution timing does not create missing basis

Assume four alternative shareholder scenarios, each with $30,000 of opening stock basis and a $40,000 ordinary loss. There are no income increases, new stock contributions, nondeductible items, prior carryforwards or special elections. The corporation has no accumulated earnings and profits. Where shown, $10,000 is assumed supported adjusted basis in a bona fide direct shareholder loan, with no repayments or restoration during the year. No loan eligibility is established by the example.

Without debt, taking $25,000 out leaves $5,000 of stock basis for the $40,000 loss. Not taking the distribution leaves $30,000 of basis, so $10,000 of the loss still fails the basis test. Merely moving the cash payment to another year does not turn $30,000 of basis into $40,000.

With the assumed $10,000 debt basis and a $25,000 distribution, $5,000 of loss uses stock basis and $10,000 uses debt basis; $25,000 remains basis-suspended. In the last alternative, the $10,000 debt basis supports a loss at the basis stage but does not shelter the $5,000 excess distribution gain.

All four current loss deductions remain unresolved after the basis stage because at-risk, passive and excess-business-loss facts are not supplied. No tax rate or tax saving is calculated. These are alternatives, not four transactions to combine on one return.

Debt repayment and transfers need their own review

The IRS basis guidance explains that repayment of debt with reduced tax basis can create taxable income. A note’s face amount is not proof that every repayment is tax-free. Reconcile the note, prior loss reductions, restoration and actual payments before determining the result.

Regulation 1.1366-2 generally makes basis-disallowed losses personal to the shareholder and permanently disallows a remaining amount on a transfer of all stock, with an exception for qualifying section 1041 transfers to a spouse or former spouse. Review final-year basis and allowed losses before applying those rules. Stock-sale gain does not automatically release basis-suspended losses or convert them into capital loss. Corporate income allocated in a sale or liquidation can affect basis through the normal adjustment rules; it is not the same as the shareholder’s stock-sale gain.

Gifts, partial transfers, complete sales and liquidations therefore require different facts. Do not recommend or reject an estate transfer solely from a headline about suspended losses.

Keep property transactions separate from shareholder basis

S status alone does not create a ban on section 1031 treatment. The real property, business/investment use, taxpayer, timing and other requirements still need review; the IRS Form 8824 instructions explain exchange reporting. An exchange of shareholder stock is not an exchange of the corporation’s real property.

Likewise, a depreciation deduction allocated by the corporation still faces the shareholder’s applicable loss limits. Entity choice does not automatically approve or prohibit cost segregation, and a basis schedule alone does not determine the best entity for owning a property.

Build a shareholder record that can be reconciled

The Form 7203 instructions identify filing situations including claimed aggregate losses, nondividend distributions, stock dispositions and shareholder loan repayments. Filing the form does not replace support for its figures. Use the edition and rules applicable to the actual return year.

Download a blank shareholder basis review record (CSV). Preserve acquisition support, opening balances, annual changes, loan records and separate loss-limitation schedules. Record missing facts rather than assuming an undocumented balance is zero.

For a related shareholder calculation, use the QBI deduction worksheet. Its return-level limitation and QBI carryforwards are separate from the stock and debt basis records.
