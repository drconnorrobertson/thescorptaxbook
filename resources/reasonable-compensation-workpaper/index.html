What Belongs in an S Corporation Reasonable Compensation File

Reasonable compensation should reflect the owner work and the business facts. A fixed salary percentage borrowed from another company does not explain the value of the services performed in your business.

Describe the actual work

Document management, production, sales, client delivery, administration, and other roles. Record time commitments and changes during the year. An owner who performs several jobs may need a role-by-role analysis rather than one generic executive title.

Collect compensation evidence

Use relevant pay information and explain why the comparisons fit the industry, location, responsibilities, and experience. Identify what income comes from owner services versus other employees or business assets. Keep the reasoning alongside the reference data.

Connect the analysis to payroll

Retain payroll reports and the timing of wages and distributions. S corporation officers providing services generally have employment tax obligations. Reclassifying wages as distributions does not remove that issue.

Review material changes

Revisit the file when the owner role, revenue model, staffing, or working time changes. A prior-year analysis is a starting point, not proof that the same salary fits indefinitely. Coordinate compensation with the broader business and tax plan.

Action checklist

  • Document roles and hours
  • Keep relevant pay comparisons
  • Explain owner-generated income
  • Retain payroll and distribution records
  • Review changes annually

Is there a universal salary-to-distribution ratio?

No. Reasonable compensation depends on the services and facts, not a universal split.
