Late S Corporation Election: What to Bring to a Tax Adviser Before Requesting Relief

If your business intended to elect S corporation status but the filing was missed or uncertain, do not assume that sending a new Form 2553 resolves the issue. Start with a factual review of eligibility, timing and prior reporting. A professional engagement should determine the available procedure before preparing a request or changing returns.

Separate a missing election from missing confirmation

Collect the signed election, mailing or fax evidence, IRS correspondence and any acceptance notice. Write down when the election was supposed to become effective and who handled the submission. If nobody can locate the records, say so rather than recreate a filing history from memory.

The Form 2553 instructions address filing, acceptance and late-election relief. An owner who cannot find an acceptance letter may have a different problem from an owner who knows nothing was filed. Ask the adviser how status will be verified and what evidence is still required.

Confirm eligibility during the relevant period

Provide formation documents, ownership history, shareholder information and the governing agreements. Include transfers, trusts or entity owners that might affect the analysis. Ask the adviser to review the business's actual eligibility rather than treating lateness as the only issue.

The IRS S corporation overview summarizes entity, shareholder and stock requirements. Late-filing relief does not mean an otherwise ineligible arrangement can be ignored. A complete review should identify any separate classification or ownership question before requesting an effective date.

Build a consistent filing history

Gather entity and shareholder returns for each affected year, extensions, payroll filings and relevant notices. Identify which returns were filed, when they were filed and how the business income was reported. Keep draft returns separate from filed versions.

The IRS late-election relief guidance describes procedures under Revenue Procedure 2013-30 and their conditions. Ask the adviser to match your facts to the applicable route, including any timing, reporting-consistency and statement requirements. Do not assume one deadline or exception covers every entity and shareholder situation.

Document the actual reason for the failure

Create a chronology of what was intended, what happened, when the problem was discovered and what was done afterward. Preserve emails, engagement records and delivery evidence where available. The request should explain the real facts relevant to reasonable cause, not use an invented story or a generic narrative that does not fit the case.

If more than one professional was involved, identify their responsibilities without assuming who made an error. The adviser preparing the request should tell you which factual statements require your confirmation and which documents support them. An accurate but incomplete record is preferable to a polished account that cannot be substantiated.

Compare the engagement by deliverables

  • An eligibility and procedure assessment for the intended effective date.
  • A list of entity and shareholder records reviewed and missing.
  • Draft forms, consents and factual statements for review.
  • Submission instructions and retained proof of filing.
  • A follow-up plan for IRS correspondence and acceptance evidence.
  • A separate scope for any return, payroll or bookkeeping correction.

Clarify what happens if the streamlined procedure is unavailable or the IRS asks for more information. The proposal should not promise relief before the facts are evaluated. Ask which additional services, deadlines and costs would require another decision.

Keep relief and operating compliance connected

Even if relief is granted, the business still needs appropriate compensation, payroll, bookkeeping and return reporting. Ask who handles those items and whether the effective date creates any follow-up work. Retain the final election documents with the permanent entity records.
