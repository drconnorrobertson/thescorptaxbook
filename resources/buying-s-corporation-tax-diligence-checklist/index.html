Buying an S Corporation: Tax Diligence to Commission Before Closing

When buying an established S corporation, the target's latest tax return is only part of the diligence file. Before signing definitive documents, have your tax adviser and transaction attorney identify the tax status, ownership restrictions, historical exposures and records you will need after closing. The review should match the actual deal rather than assume every acquisition of an S corporation has the same tax result.

Clarify the transaction before asking for a tax estimate

State whether you propose to buy stock, buy business assets or use another negotiated structure. Identify the buyer, financing arrangements, sellers, closing timetable and any building included in the transaction. Give the tax adviser the term sheet and draft agreements rather than only a purchase price.

Ask which structural alternatives the review will compare. If a special tax election is being considered, require a separate explanation of eligibility, required consents, filing obligations and the consequences to both sides. Do not describe a stock purchase as providing the same tax basis result as an asset purchase without transaction-specific analysis.

Verify the S election and ownership history

Request the election documents, evidence of acceptance if available, historical returns and a complete ownership history. Ask the adviser to investigate inconsistencies between those records. Filing an S corporation return does not replace a review of whether the corporation met the relevant conditions throughout the period under examination.

The IRS S corporation overview identifies limits involving shareholder eligibility and stock structure. Make the proposed buyer and any holding entity explicit. The new ownership structure must be reviewed before closing, not discovered during preparation of the next return.

Review tax exposures beyond the income-tax return

The request list should cover payroll, employment-tax filings, owner compensation, state registrations, notices and open examinations relevant to the acquisition. Ask who will evaluate sales and other transaction taxes where applicable. If a category is excluded from the tax diligence engagement, assign it elsewhere rather than leave it unowned.

For owner-operated companies, compare payroll records with distributions and the services the owners performed. IRS compensation guidance explains why distributions do not eliminate reasonable-compensation requirements. The buyer's diligence should identify a potential exposure for professional review rather than assume the seller's reporting is correct.

Keep property and shareholder balances visible

Determine whether the operating company owns its premises or leases from a seller-controlled entity. Obtain the property asset schedule or lease and identify any planned purchase, transfer or replacement arrangement. An acquisition of the operating business should not leave the buyer uncertain about who owns the building or what the continuing occupancy costs will be.

Request shareholder loan records and related-party balances, including how each will be treated at closing. The seller's personal stock-basis schedule is not automatically the buyer's opening basis record. IRS basis guidance explains the distinction between stock and debt basis; the buyer needs documentation tied to their own acquisition and financing.

Ask for a prioritized findings report

  • Confirmed facts: the documents reviewed and periods covered.
  • Unresolved questions: missing records and assumptions that affect the conclusion.
  • Closing issues: matters that need a decision or additional diligence before completion.
  • Contract coordination: findings for the transaction attorney to consider in the agreement.
  • Post-closing work: opening balances, payroll transition, return responsibilities and filing deadlines.

Distinguish a preliminary screen from a detailed tax diligence engagement. A short review of recent returns cannot establish that all historical liabilities have been found. Ask which findings change the modeled economics and which can be addressed through a defined post-closing task.

Connect diligence to the first year of ownership

Agree on who prepares returns for the closing year and how information will be shared between sellers and buyers. Have the adviser explain any ownership-change allocation and election questions applicable to the transaction. Preserve final agreements, closing statements and tax workpapers together so the first return is not reconstructed from scattered emails.

Share confidential agreements through the agreed secure process. Tax diligence is one component of a purchase decision and does not certify the business's value or eliminate acquisition risk.
