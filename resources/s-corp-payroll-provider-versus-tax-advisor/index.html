S Corp Payroll Service or Tax Adviser? Divide the Work Before Year-End

An S corporation owner can have a payroll service and still lack a complete year-end compensation review. Processing paychecks, choosing a supported owner salary, coordinating benefits and preparing the income-tax return are different jobs. Use this guide to decide which responsibilities belong in each provider's engagement before the last payroll and annual forms are finalized.

Start with a responsibility list

List the people responsible for payroll processing, bookkeeping, corporate tax, personal tax and retirement-plan administration. One firm may perform several roles, but the scope should still say which work is included. Do not assume that access to the same software means the providers have agreed to exchange instructions.

The IRS discussion of outsourced payroll explains that the employer generally remains responsible for payroll tax obligations, with important distinctions among arrangements. Ask what type of provider you are using and how you will verify filings and deposits. Outsourcing should create a reliable process, not remove the owner's visibility.

Separate a salary decision from a payroll run

A payroll processor can often run the wage amount you supply. That does not necessarily mean it has analyzed whether the amount is reasonable for your services. Give the tax adviser a description of duties, time worked, business results, other employees and distributions, then request a documented compensation recommendation appropriate to the facts.

IRS S corporation compensation guidance addresses shareholder-employees and reasonable compensation. Avoid treating a fixed salary-to-distribution percentage as a universal rule. The provider proposal should distinguish calculation, documentation, payroll entry and final verification.

Coordinate owner health insurance before forms are final

Identify the shareholder-employees, ownership information and premiums paid or reimbursed by the corporation. Ask the tax adviser which amounts need payroll reporting and have the payroll provider confirm how the instruction will be implemented. Retain the payment records and the resulting payroll report together.

Special rules apply to health insurance for greater-than-2-percent shareholder-employees. The IRS compensation and medical-insurance guidance explains reporting and deduction conditions. A payroll entry alone does not establish eligibility for a deduction on the owner's return. Make the personal-return review a separate assigned step rather than assume the W-2 resolves every question.

Give retirement and reimbursement questions an owner

Provide retirement-plan documents and year-to-date contribution records to the appropriate adviser or administrator. Ask who checks compensation inputs, contribution instructions and any plan-specific deadlines. The payroll provider should receive clear authorized instructions rather than infer a contribution target from the owner's desired deduction.

Also reconcile owner reimbursements and business expenses paid personally. Distinguish payroll items from properly documented reimbursement arrangements and unresolved bookkeeping entries. If the year-end review uncovers missing documentation, ask how it should be addressed before final reporting instead of forcing every payment into the same payroll category.

Compare service proposals using a handoff checklist

  • Inputs: who supplies ownership, benefits, distributions and year-to-date records?
  • Advice: who decides the tax treatment and documents the reasoning?
  • Processing: who enters approved changes and confirms the affected pay periods?
  • Review: who checks the payroll register, annual forms and bookkeeping reconciliation?
  • Corrections: who investigates an error, files a correction and tells the tax-return preparer?
  • Fees: which advisory, processing, amendment and follow-up services are included?

A less expensive processing plan may suit an owner who already has an adviser coordinating the decisions. A bundled arrangement may be helpful when it genuinely includes that review. Compare the actual work and communication process rather than assuming either arrangement is automatically better.

Use a completion checklist, not an email assumption

Before year-end, obtain the provider's processing cutoff and agree on a review date early enough to resolve questions. After changes are processed, compare the final reports with the written instructions. Keep evidence of filing and payment, and give the tax preparer the final version rather than an earlier draft.
