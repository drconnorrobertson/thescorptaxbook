S Corporation Basis: A Year-End Reconciliation Workflow

An S corporation shareholder basis schedule is a cumulative record. Reconcile it each year using actual contributions, income, losses, distributions, and loan activity instead of estimating basis from the company bank balance.

Start with the prior closing balance

Obtain the prior basis schedule and confirm it agrees with the historical returns and transactions. Identify any unsupported opening amounts. A current K-1 cannot explain every contribution or distribution from earlier years.

Separate stock and debt activity

Track equity contributions and distributions separately from direct shareholder lending and repayments. Debt basis can affect loss deductions, but it does not make a non-dividend distribution tax-free. Loan guarantees and entity liabilities need review rather than automatic inclusion.

Apply the relevant limitations

The IRS identifies basis, at-risk, passive activity, and excess business loss limitations for S corporation shareholders. Adequate basis alone does not ensure that a loss can be deducted currently. Retain carryforward schedules for amounts limited at different stages.

Reconcile before a large distribution

Use current books for a preliminary review, then finalize the schedule with actual tax-year reporting. Document the owner decision and accounting treatment. If income or expenses change before filing, revisit the distribution analysis.

Action checklist

  • Verify the prior closing basis
  • Track stock and debt separately
  • Reconcile the K-1 and owner ledger
  • Retain limited-loss schedules
  • Review major distributions before payment

Does a business loan always increase shareholder basis?

No. S corporation debt basis generally requires qualifying shareholder debt. The actual lending arrangement and applicable rules need review.
